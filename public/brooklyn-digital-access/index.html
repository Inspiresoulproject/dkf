Why this matters

A computer can change how a child learns.

For many students, access to a dependable computer shapes what they can do after the school day ends — from homework and online research to building digital skills and exploring future opportunities.

Our initiative is being developed to help close that gap through responsible device donations and community-led outreach, beginning with children in Brooklyn who face financial barriers to owning a suitable laptop.

A responsible, community-first process

From donated laptop to learning opportunity.

We are building the program around clear records, safe equipment preparation, and fair selection of recipients.

01

Secure equipment

Invite businesses and supporters to contribute usable laptops, chargers, and related technology.

02

Prepare each device

Arrange secure data sanitization, inventory, testing, and necessary refurbishment before distribution.

03

Work with the community

Collaborate with Brooklyn organizations to introduce eligible families to the program when applications open.

04

Distribute & follow up

Review applications fairly, complete guardian-authorized handovers, and track educational impact with care.

Designed for learning needs

Supporting children who need access most.

DKF is developing needs-based eligibility criteria for children in Brooklyn. Our intended focus includes:

  • Households facing financial barriers to buying a computer
  • Students without reliable access to a suitable device for learning
  • Families applying with parent or guardian participation
  • A fair and documented review process as equipment becomes available
Notice for families

Applications aren't open yet.

We don't have an announced application opening date. Once donations, preparation resources, and community arrangements are in place, we'll share eligibility details and next steps on DKF's official website. We cannot promise laptops before supply is secured.

Helpful information

Frequently asked questions.

Questions about donating equipment or becoming a partner? Our team is available at help@digitalkidsfoundation.org.

Are 40–50 laptops already available?

No. This is the target for our first pilot. We're currently seeking equipment donations and community partnerships.

Can a bank or company donate retired laptops?

Yes. We welcome inquiries about surplus devices. Any transfer should follow the donor's approved IT asset disposal and data sanitization requirements, agreed before handover.

Is DKF accepting family applications?

Not for this initiative yet. The application process will be announced when laptop supply and program arrangements are sufficiently ready.

How will recipients be selected?

We plan to use a consistent needs-based review, involving parents or guardians when appropriate. We will not ask referral partners to share children's private information without appropriate consent.

Can a community organization participate?

Yes. Brooklyn youth organizations, after-school programs, libraries, and community partners can contact us to discuss referrals, outreach, and potential event coordination.
